*Menus are not tests. They are clues arranged by cooking method.*

The most useful Japanese menu vocabulary describes how food is prepared. Once you recognize those building blocks, a menu stops being a wall of kanji. {{お品書き|おしながき|oshinagaki · menu}} is the formal word you may see in a restaurant; *menu* is also widely understood.

## Cooking words

{{焼き|やき|yaki · grilled / cooked}} appears in *yakitori* (grilled chicken), *okonomiyaki*, and *yakizakana* (grilled fish). {{揚げ|あげ|age · fried}} appears in *karaage* and *agemono*. *Mushi* means steamed, *yude* boiled, and *nama* raw or fresh. These pieces may appear before or after the ingredient.

## The meal formats

{{定食|ていしょく|teishoku · set meal}} usually includes a main dish, rice, soup, and pickles. *Don* means a bowl of rice topped with something: *oyakodon* has chicken and egg, *gyuudon* beef. *Mori-awase* is an assortment, while *tabehoudai* means all-you-can-eat.

## Ingredients worth recognizing

The same few words appear across many menus. *Niku* is meat, *sakana* fish, and *yasai* vegetables. *Tori* means chicken, *buta* pork, and *gyuu* beef. *Miso* is fermented soybean paste, *shio* is salt, and *tare* is a prepared sauce. If you see *gohan*, it may mean cooked rice or a meal in general; context does the work.

Menus also signal serving style. *Hiyashi* means chilled, *atsumori* means hot noodles served hot, and *kake* indicates broth poured over noodles. *Kakiage* is a mixed vegetable-and-seafood fritter, while *shio-yaki* means grilled with salt rather than a sweet soy glaze. Learning these pieces lets you choose by texture and seasoning, not only by photographs.

When a menu lists *shun no osusume*, it is pointing to the chef’s seasonal recommendation. That phrase is useful because it invites the staff to explain. A good question is *kore wa donna aji desu ka?* (“What kind of flavor is this?”). Japanese restaurant vocabulary becomes much easier once you treat the menu as a conversation starter.

## Specials and uncertainty

{{季節限定|きせつげんてい|kisetsu gentei · seasonal limited}} means the item is available for a season or while supplies last. *Osusume* is the recommendation. If a word is unfamiliar, point and ask *kore wa nan desu ka?* The answer may be a miniature food lesson, and staff are used to this question.
