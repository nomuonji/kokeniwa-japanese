*You do not need fluent Japanese to move through Japan. You need landmarks in language.*

Start with signs. {{駅|えき|eki · station}}, {{入口|いりぐち|iriguchi · entrance}}, and {{出口|でぐち|deguchi · exit}} appear everywhere. *Toire* is the casual word for toilet; *o-tearai* is the polite sign. A little vocabulary turns a wall of kanji into a map.

## Stations and getting around

*Kippu* is a ticket, *kaado* is a card, and *norikae* is a transfer. If you are lost, ask *eki wa doko desu ka?* (“Where is the station?”) or *kore wa Shibuya ni ikimasu ka?* (“Does this go to Shibuya?”). *Koko* means here, *soko* there near the listener, and *asoko* over there.

On a platform, *ホーム* (*hoomu*) means platform, not a house. *Kaisatsu* is the ticket gate. *Kakunin* means confirmation, a word you may hear when staff check a route or reservation.

## Hotels and luggage

At check-in, *chekku-in shimasu* is understood. {{荷物|にもつ|nimotsu · luggage}} can be left with *azukete moraemasu ka?* (“May I leave it with you?”). *Yooyaku* is a reservation, *pasupooto* a passport, and *kagi* a key. Hotel staff may ask for your name as *onamae wa?* — the polite “your name?”

## The phrase that unlocks help

When you need assistance, say *sumimasen* first. Then use a short question and point to the place or screen. *Wakarimasu ka?* asks “Do you understand?”; *wakarimasen* means “I do not understand.” Add *mou ichido onegaishimasu* (“one more time, please”) and you can keep the conversation kind even when your grammar collapses.

## Small words on signs

*Kita* and *minami* mean north and south; *higashi* and *nishi* mean east and west. *Chika* is underground, *chijō* (地上) is above ground, and *kaidan* means stairs. At a station, *ban-sen* is platform number, *houmen* (方面) indicates the direction of travel, and *jiyuu-seki* means a non-reserved seat. These are not words you need to produce fluently. Recognizing them while walking is enough.

For emergencies, *tasukete* means “help,” *byouin* is hospital, and *keisatsu* is police. Japan is safe, but knowing these words lowers the cost of asking. Keep your accommodation name written in Japanese, especially when taking a taxi. Showing a screen with the address and saying *koko made onegaishimasu* (“to here, please”) is a perfectly competent travel interaction.
